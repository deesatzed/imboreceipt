## 1. The Alien Goggles Report

An extraterrestrial statistician, given our literature and our datasets, would file four observations:

**Observation 1: "You are optimizing the decoder while the channel is the bottleneck."**
Humans treat rare-event prediction as a *model* problem. But the mutual information $I(Y; X_{\text{observed}})$ between the outcome and what is actually measured is fixed before any model is trained. Thousands of papers adjust the decoder (loss functions, resampling, latent-space GANs); almost none fund the channel (what gets measured, what gets labeled, when). The alien finds this like watching a species try to hear better by evolving bigger ears while ignoring that the sound isn't being emitted.

**Observation 2: "You named the disease after its symptom."**
"Imbalanced learning" names a *frequency property* of labels, not a mechanism. A 0.1%-prevalence problem can be nearly deterministic (excellent prediction possible) or nearly stochastic (nothing possible). Grouping them into one research field is like grouping coughs and broken legs under "problems that hurt." The controlled experiments confirm this: when researchers manipulate imbalance ratio and class overlap independently, **overlap damages minority recognition far more than ratio does** — imbalance controls how *often* you see informative points; overlap controls whether the points are informative *at all* (Lango & Stefanowski 2022; the 2026 "Beyond Imbalance Ratio" study found separability moderates method effectiveness at $\rho = -0.72$ while IR itself correlates *negatively* with oversampling benefit).

**Observation 3: "Your thermometer is noisier than your fevers."**
This is the deepest reason there are no front-runners. With $n_+ = 50$ test positives, the standard error of estimated recall at $r = 0.8$ is $\sqrt{r(1-r)/n_+} \approx 0.057$. Typical claimed improvements in this literature are +0.01 to +0.03 AUPRC. **The field's measurement instrument has more variance than its effect sizes.** A field in that regime *cannot* converge — winners are drawn from noise, replication fails silently, and the leaderboard is a random number generator with authorship. Vision/NLP converged because they had (a) a shared reusable representation, (b) a scaling lever that kept paying, and (c) test sets big enough to resolve differences. Rare-event tabular learning has none of the three: no shared schema, no way to conjure more positives, and evaluation sets that can't adjudicate.

**Observation 4: "Your labels are not events. They are receipts."**
The observed label encodes an *observation policy*: diagnosed disease depends on who got tested; detected fraud depends on who got investigated; recorded churn depends on who got a retention offer. Humans benchmark algorithms against a target that is partially unidentified — the model may be learning the institution's investigation policy rather than the risk. No algorithm comparison can resolve an unidentified target. The alien would consider this disqualifying and would audit the label-generating process before reading a single methods paper.

## 2. First Principles: The Information Accounting

Everything reduces to a ledger with four accounts. Every failure in rare-event prediction draws from exactly one (or more) of them, and **each account has a different, non-substitutable remedy**:

| Account | What it is | Only remedy | Can resampling/loss tricks touch it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Information limit** | Bayes error given measured $X$: $R^* = \mathbb{E}[\min(\eta(X), 1-\eta(X))]$, $\eta(x)=P(Y{=}1\mid x)$ | New features $Z$ with $I(Y; Z \mid X) > 0$ | **No** |
| **Estimation limit** | Excess risk $\sim O(\sqrt{\mathcal{C}/n_+})$ — governed by *absolute* positive count, not the ratio | More positives, pooling, priors, regularization | Marginally (as priors) |
| **Label limit** | Ascertainment noise: unrecorded positives among "negatives" (PU structure), mislabels, horizon censoring | Label adjudication, PU methods, hazard reformulation | **No** |
| **Decision limit** | Base-rate arithmetic + cost structure | Threshold/utility design, abstention, cascades | Only trivially (reweighting ≈ moving the threshold) |

Two theorems do most of the work:

**(a) The base-rate ceiling is free to compute and brutal.** At prevalence $\pi = 0.001$, even a superb classifier (TPR $0.8$, FPR $0.01$) yields precision

$$\text{PPV} = \frac{\pi \cdot \text{TPR}}{\pi \cdot \text{TPR} + (1-\pi)\cdot \text{FPR}} \approx 7.4\%.$$

Most "modeling failures" in deployed rare-event systems are this arithmetic being misread as algorithmic inadequacy. If the use case needs PPV ≥ 30%, you need specificity ≈ 0.998 — and asking whether *any plausible feature set* could deliver that is the most decisive five-minute analysis in the entire field.

**(b) Synthetic data cannot create information — correctly stated.** The council flagged a common sloppy invocation of the Data Processing Inequality here, so let me state it precisely: synthetic samples are a function of the training data plus independent randomness, so they add **zero independent evidence about the population**. What they *can* do is inject an inductive bias (a smoothing prior). This is why the empirical record looks the way it does: *"To SMOTE, or not to SMOTE?"* (Elor & Averbuch-Elor) found balancing helps weak learners but **does not improve tuned XGBoost/CatBoost**, and threshold optimization matches balancing at a fraction of the compute. Latent-space expansion (VAE/GAN/diffusion augmentation) is the same trade with more GPU: its gains are capped by the quality of the prior it smuggles in, and it can actively harm you by interpolating *across incompatible positive subtypes* — because one "rare class" is usually five rarer mechanisms (the Anna Karenina structure: healthy systems are alike; each failure fails in its own way).

**So: is defining the ceiling more vital than latent-space expansion? Yes — with one precise exception.** If the learning curve in $n_+$ is still steep (estimation-limited), priors and augmentation are legitimate. If it's flat (information- or label-limited), every dollar spent on decoder gymnastics is burned. You cannot know which regime you're in without the audit. Ceiling-first isn't aesthetics; it's the branch condition of the whole program.

## 3. Second Principles: What "The Ceiling" Actually Is

Three corrections keep the ceiling idea honest (this is where naive ceiling-first programs die):

1. **There are three ceilings, not one**: the information ceiling given $X$, the statistical ceiling given $n_+$, and the operational ceiling given costs/alert capacity/drift. Report a *decomposition*, not a single falsely precise percentage.
2. **The asymmetry of proof**: a good achieved score demonstrates attainability; a bad score does *not* establish a ceiling. All data-driven ceiling estimates are ceilings **given the current channel** — they say nothing about headroom from unmeasured variables. Two data-generating processes can produce identical observed distributions while differing radically in whether an unmeasured biomarker makes the outcome predictable. **Unmeasured headroom is non-identifiable from $X$ alone.** This is precisely why Part 1 (ceilings) and Part 2 (your missing-variable hunt) are *the same program*: the only way to estimate the headroom above the local ceiling is a cheap, targeted pilot measurement — which is your instinct #1, formalized.
3. **The label ceiling is partially estimable, not mystical.** Under class-conditional noise, observed AUC is an affine shrinkage of true AUC toward 0.5 (Menon et al. 2015); PU machinery (Elkan–Noto, nnPU) can estimate the class prior under anchor assumptions; a blinded adjudication of ~100–200 stratified cases (high-scoring negatives, low-scoring positives, random controls) bounds the rest. And one reformulation recovers information the binary label discards: **many "false positives" are future positives**. Recasting the task as discrete-time hazard / survival modeling converts censoring from noise into signal — a move essentially absent from the imbalanced-learning literature.

<details>
<summary><strong>Estimation toolkit and its failure modes (expand for practice)</strong></summary>

| Estimator | Mechanism | Sharpest failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence/PPV arithmetic | Closed form, free | None — do it first, always |
| Learning curves in $n_+$ | Fit $L(n_+) = L_\infty + a\,n_+^{-b}$ on subsampled positives | Functional-form misspecification; phase changes when rare subtypes appear only at larger $n$; unstable below ~100 positives |
| Cover–Hart kNN bounds | $R^* \le R_{1NN} \le 2R^*(1-R^*)$ | Asymptotic; distance concentration in high-$d$ mixed tabular data |
| Henze–Penrose / Friedman–Rafsky MST divergence bounds | Cross-class edges in a minimum spanning tree bound Bayes error *without fitting a classifier* (Berisha et al. 2016) | Metric arbitrariness; noise columns bias bounds; majority-class domination |
| Matched-set collision audit | Match positives to near-identical negatives; local prevalence directly observes overlap | Empty neighborhoods unless matching on domain-selected core variables |
| Blinded label adjudication | Stratified re-review estimate of $(\hat\alpha, \hat\beta)$ | Circularity if adjudicators see the model score; noise rates unidentifiable without assumptions |
| Sample-size adequacy | Riley et al. `pmsampsize` events-per-parameter criteria | Treats clustered/repeated observations as independent |

Treat all of these as **rough bounds with uncertainty, never point estimates**. The deliverable is a *Headroom Index* with a confidence interval:

$$H = \frac{M_{\text{achieved}} - M_{\text{chance}}}{\hat{M}_{\text{ceiling} \mid X} - M_{\text{chance}}}$$

where $M_{\text{chance}}$ for AUPRC is the prevalence $\pi$. $H \approx 1$ with a flat learning curve = stop modeling, expand the channel. $H \ll 1$ with a steep curve = acquire positives/pool, don't hunt features yet.
</details>

## 4. Your Three Instincts, Stress-Tested

### Instinct 1 — Unmeasured features driving FP/FN more than TP: **strongest direction, but needs an etiology triage first**

This is the only mathematically valid way to lower the information ceiling: acquire $Z$ with $I(Y; Z \mid X) > 0$. Your specific twist — that hidden drivers concentrate in the *error regions* rather than uniformly — is sound and is the targeting mechanism the field lacks. But here is the trap that has killed most prior attempts (and that you correctly sensed): **an error is not evidence of a missing variable until competing etiologies are excluded.** A false positive can be (in order of prevalence in real systems):

1. A **future positive** (horizon censoring) → remedy: hazard reformulation
2. An **unrecorded positive** (PU structure) → remedy: label adjudication
3. **Aleatoric overlap** (irreducible given any features) → remedy: nothing; abstain
4. **Estimation variance** → remedy: more positives
5. **Missing information** → *your hunt applies only here*

Mining errors without this triage yields narrative overfitting on 25 audited cases, regression to the mean, and hindsight leakage (variables recorded *because* someone already suspected the event — e.g., a lactate test ordered because sepsis was suspected). The fixes are known and cheap: **cross-fitted predictions** (never discover hypotheses on in-fold errors), **matched-pair designs at fixed model score** $\hat\eta(X)$ (compare $Y{=}1$ vs $Y{=}0$ cases the model scored identically — this controls the selection effect of conditioning on errors), **correct-prediction controls**, and validation on untouched temporal data. When the hidden driver is genuinely unmeasurable, **proximal causal inference** (Miao, Tchetgen Tchetgen) gives you a formal fallback: two independent proxy types (a treatment-side proxy $Z$ and an outcome-side proxy $W$) can identify effects *through* the unmeasured variable without ever observing it. This is the rigorous version of your intuition, and it's barely been touched by the imbalanced-learning community.

### Instinct 2 — Chaos/randomness/game theory ensembles: **split the bundle; keep two, demote one**

- **Game theory: legitimate, already load-bearing.** Boosting *is* a repeated zero-sum game solved via the minimax theorem (Freund–Schapire). The right modern forms: **Neyman–Pearson classification** (hard FPR constraint, minimize misses — the natural formalization of "sensitivity under an alert budget" that the F1 literature never names), and **Stackelberg/DRO** formulations *if and only if* the positive class is strategic (fraud, intrusion) or drifting. Known failure mode: worst-case conservatism explodes false positives at low prevalence — DRO must be gated on out-of-time utility, not adopted on principle.
- **Randomness: legitimate, but the governing math is the bias–variance–covariance and ambiguity decompositions** (Ueda–Nakano; Krogh–Vedelsby): ensemble gain = individual competence minus error *covariance*. The novel move your intuition points toward: **diversify over mechanism hypotheses, not seeds**. Members trained on different candidate feature sets from Instinct 3, evaluated with Shapley-style marginal contribution, form a council whose *disagreement localizes missing information* — disagreement becomes the acquisition signal for Instinct 1. That closes a loop no one is running.
- **Chaos theory: mostly metaphor on i.i.d. tabular rows — and I say this as the person you asked to explore it.** Strange attractors and Lyapunov exponents have no identifiable role on cross-sectional patient rows. The *honest* residue is real but narrow: for genuinely dynamical, partially observed processes (ICU waveforms, bearing vibration, account activity streams), **critical slowing down** — rising variance and lag-1 autocorrelation before regime shifts (Scheffer et al. 2009) — is a validated, transferable early-warning family, and forecast-horizon limits from dynamics tell you when a longer history cannot help. Use chaos where there is an actual dynamical system; elsewhere it's vocabulary, not mechanism. An ensemble cannot exceed the Bayes risk given $X$ no matter how it's dressed — council members sharing the same omitted predictors share the same blindness.

### Instinct 3 — LLM cross-domain influence mining: **powerful generator, dangerous evidence**

This is literature-based discovery (Swanson's ABC model, 1986) at machine scale, and CAAFE/REFeat already show LLM feature engineering works on tabular data. Your second/third-principles framing contains the key upgrade: **transfer at the level of invariant mathematical motifs, not surface semantics.** "Fraud and sepsis are both rare" is worthless; "both are regime shifts in a stressed networked system, so point-process burstiness, contagion topology, and variance-inflation-under-load features should transfer" is a testable structural claim. The council's falsifiable version: *motif-level transfers should replicate at >3× the rate of semantic-analogy transfers.* The leashes required: an **observability filter** (every candidate must map to a transform of data you already hold, or an acquirable measurement with cost below its expected value of information), a **candidate budget** $K \le \lfloor n_+/20 \rfloor$ with FDR control (200 LLM candidates tested against 100 positives is a spurious-discovery factory), and **equal-budget comparison against expert and conventional-retrieval baselines** — otherwise you're measuring "more brainstorming," not an LLM advantage.

## 5. The Breakthrough Scaffolding: **HEADROOM**

The unit of optimization shifts from *loss functions on fixed $X$* to **information triage: which dollar buys labels, examples, measurements, or algorithms — and did the diagnosis predict the win?** Five layers, each with a gate that can *stop* the wrong kind of work.

```
L0 CONTRACT → L1 CEILING AUDIT → L2 CHANNEL EXPANSION → L3 COUNCIL → L4 DEPLOYMENT LOOP
                    ↑______________________ re-audit after every channel change ______________|
```

**L0 — Contract (the decision, not the dataset).**
Fix the action, cost matrix $(c_{01}, c_{10})$, alert capacity, prediction horizon, and a label definition knowable at prediction time. Compute the required operating point via the PPV formula.
*Gate G0:* if required specificity is implausible for any realistic feature set → redesign (narrower high-prevalence population, two-stage cascade, different action). Most projects should die here and don't.

**L1 — Ceiling Audit (your Part 1, operationalized).**
Calibrated GBDT + penalized GLM on the *natural* distribution, proper losses, temporal splits, bootstrap CIs resampled over positives. Then: prevalence arithmetic → learning curves in $n_+$ → overlap bounds → blinded label audit (incl. PU prior estimation) → Headroom Index with CI.
*Gate G1, three-way branch:* steep curve → **estimation-limited** (pool data, priors, augmentation-as-regularizer — the one legitimate home for your latent-space tools); heavy audited noise → **label-limited** (adjudicate, PU/hazard reformulation); flat curve + clean labels + below target → **information-limited** → L2, and *freeze resampling/loss experiments permanently*.

**L2 — Channel Expansion (your instincts 1 + 3, fused into one loop).**
1. **Error anatomy:** cross-fitted, confident, *model-family-consistent* errors only; matched pairs at fixed $\hat\eta(X)$; blinded triage into the five etiologies. Proceed only if a meaningful share is "missing information."
2. **Side-channel probe (the cheap decisive test):** can blinded humans/LLMs, using existing free-text notes, event logs, transcripts — but not $Y$ — distinguish matched positive/negative pairs better than chance (McNemar $p<0.01$)? If yes, the signal already sits in your institution's logs: extract, don't instrument.
3. **Motif-level LLM mining:** strip domain nouns from the failure modes, encode as abstract motifs (burstiness, resource depletion, brokerage shift, critical slowing down), retrieve cross-domain predictors *conditioned on those motifs*, apply the observability filter and budget $K$.
4. **Case-cohort pilot economics:** measure candidate $Z$ only on all $n_+$ positives + $\sim 4n_+$ score-matched controls (≈0.5% of full-cohort cost at $\pi=0.001$); conditional logistic regression / model-X conditional randomization tests project the population-level utility gain *before* building pipelines. Keep only FDR-surviving candidates with pre-registered gain in proper score on untouched temporal data; if $Z$ is unmeasurable, attempt proximal identification via proxy pairs.
*Gate G2:* re-run L1. **The ceiling itself must move.** This is the metric no imbalanced-learning paper reports.

**L3 — Council (your instinct 2, disciplined).**
Members diversified over *validated mechanism hypotheses* from L2, not seeds; simple or Shapley-weighted aggregation (learned weights overfit tiny positive validation sets); Neyman–Pearson constraint on FPR; DRO/Stackelberg only for strategic positives, retained only if out-of-time utility beats ERM within the FPR budget. Council disagreement in the uncertainty band $[\tau_{\text{low}}, \tau_{\text{high}}]$ triggers **active acquisition** of expensive $Z$ (two-stage cascade) and feeds new matched pairs back to L2. Conformal abstention where the ceiling is below the utility target — "I don't know" is a prediction the current literature refuses to make.

**L4 — Deployment loop.**
Prospective silent evaluation → impact study; monitor drift and **performativity** (blocking the card censors the fraud label; treating the patient censors the decompensation label — your ground truth decays *because the model works*). Every deployment change re-opens the L1 audit.

H1. Receipt decomposition (ecology: capture–recapture and occupancy models)

Nature's solution: Ecologists never treat "not seen" as "absent". They model P(sighting) = P(present) × P(detected | present) and separate the two with repeat or independent surveys.

Nature's solution: Organisms spend expensive sensing where uncertainty times stakes is highest, and they keep some exploratory sampling.
Hypothesis: Routing a costly measurement only to cases near the decision boundary, plus a small random audit, expands the channel and de-biases the receipts at a fraction of the cost of measuring everyone.
Application: Triggered lab tests, manual review queues, inspection scheduling.

H3. Two-signal rule (immunology costimulation; coincidence detection in physics)

Nature's solution: A T cell needs antigen recognition and a separate danger signal before it fires. Physics detectors require coincident hits in independent instruments.
Hypothesis: The precision ceiling is broken by a second channel whose false positives are independent of the first among true negatives, not by a better single score. The gain is predictable in advance from the false-positive correlation.
Worked example: A second independent test with the same TPR 0.8 and FPR 0.01 moves PPV from about 7.4% to about 86%, while recall drops to 0.64.
Time as the second signal: Kinetic proofreading (Hopfield) uses repeated delayed checks to multiply specificity. Re-observation after a delay does the same wherever the cost of waiting is tolerable.
Application: Sepsis alerts (vitals plus biomarker), fraud (transaction plus device or behavior), predictive maintenance (vibration plus thermal).
Kill test: Measure false-positive correlation between the channels on negatives. If it is high, the gain disappears.

Nature's solution: Evolution did not make cough, fever or fear accurate. It made false alarms cheap and tuned the threshold to the cost asymmetry.
Hypothesis: In many deployments, lowering the cost of a false positive through graded, reversible responses yields more utility than any achievable gain in discrimination.
Application: Step-up authentication instead of a block, a watch-list instead of admission, a cheap inspection instead of a shutdown.
Kill test: Run a utility simulation comparing a halved false-positive cost against a +0.03 AUPRC gain.
Estimation account: more effective positives

H5. Learn the self, not the enemy (thymic negative selection)

Nature's solution: The immune system never trains on pathogen examples. It models "self" exhaustively and reacts to deviation.
Hypothesis: Below a critical number of positives, a density model of normals with few-shot calibration beats discriminative learning. The crossover point is predictable from the positive count and class overlap.
Application: Novel fraud types, zero-day intrusions, first-of-kind equipment failures.

Nature's solution: B cells mutate variants of a rare hit, and only variants that pass selection against the real antigen survive.

# CREDO: The Breakthrough Hypothesis for Rare-Event Learning

Your **HEADROOM** draft is a strong research program, but it is not yet a singular, defensible breakthrough hypothesis. A winning hypothesis needs:

1. one proposed causal mechanism for stagnation;
2. one new measurable quantity;
3. one architecture implied by that mechanism; and
4. one experiment capable of killing the idea.

After checking literature available through **November 16, 2026**, I would center the program on the following.

> ## The CREDO Hypothesis
> **Rare-event prediction is constrained primarily by a local deficit of likelihood-ratio evidence at the operational decision boundary—not by class imbalance itself.**
>
> After repairing policy-generated labels, the highest-value improvements will come from acquiring or constructing measurements that:
>
> 1. provide new conditional evidence specifically within stable false-positive and false-negative regions;
> 2. are weakly dependent on the existing channel’s false-positive mechanisms; and
> 3. correspond to distinct event-generating mechanisms.
>
> Consequently, an **OR-of-ANDs evidence lattice**—independent confirmation within each mechanism and rescue across heterogeneous mechanisms—should shift the attainable precision–recall frontier more per positive example and per dollar than resampling, latent-space augmentation, larger monolithic models, or seed-diverse ensembles.

**CREDO** stands for:

- **C**ontract the decision
- **R**epair the receipt
- **E**stimate the evidence frontier
- **D**iscover residual channels
- **O**rthogonalize the evidence architecture

This is stronger and more novel than “ceiling-first” alone.

---

## 1. The corrected alien diagnosis

The alien would not call this field “imbalanced learning.” It would call it:

> **Decision-making with insufficient, selectively observed, and correlated evidence.**

Three factors explain the absence of durable front-runners.

### 1.1 Label frequency is not the governing mechanism

The same 0.1% prevalence can describe:

- a nearly deterministic event with an excellent biomarker;
- a heterogeneous mixture of unrelated failures;
- a policy-detected event with many hidden positives;
- a dynamical transition with precursors;
- or an exogenous shock that is intrinsically unpredictable.

Empirical work has repeatedly found that noise, overlap, small disjuncts, and minority heterogeneity can be more damaging than the global imbalance ratio. Strong tabular learners also frequently gain little from generic oversampling once thresholding and tuning are handled properly.

### 1.2 The target is often not the event

Let:

- \(Y\) = the true event;
- \(O\) = whether the event was observable, tested, investigated, or allowed to occur;
- \(\tilde Y\) = the recorded label.

Then operational data often contain:

\[
\tilde Y = h(Y,O,\text{measurement error}),
\]

not \(Y\) itself.

Selective-label research shows that evaluation can be biased when observed outcomes depend on prior human decisions. That means a model can learn who was tested or investigated rather than who truly had the event.

### 1.3 The literature optimizes global fit instead of local evidence

A feature can have almost no effect on global AUC or log loss yet be extraordinarily valuable inside the top 0.1% of scores where decisions occur.

Conversely, a feature can improve global discrimination while contributing almost nothing to the specific false positives that make deployment unusable.

That is the missing conceptual shift:

> **The value of a feature is conditional on the decision region and the evidence already present.**

# 2. First principle: calculate the evidence deficit

Posterior odds equal prior odds multiplied by the likelihood ratio:

\[
\frac{P(Y=1\mid X=x)}{P(Y=0\mid X=x)}
=
\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}
\cdot
\frac{p(x\mid Y=1)}{p(x\mid Y=0)}.
\]

If the required precision is \(q\), the observed evidence must supply a likelihood ratio of at least:

\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{required}}
=
\frac{q/(1-q)}{\pi/(1-\pi)}.
\]

Define the **base-rate evidence deficit** for an instance:

\[
D_q(x)
=
\left[
\operatorname{logit}(q)
-
\operatorname{logit}(\pi)
-
\log \Lambda_X(x)
\right]_+.
\]

This is the amount of additional log evidence needed before an action is justified.

## Example

At:

- prevalence \(\pi=0.001\);
- desired precision \(q=0.30\);

the required total likelihood ratio is approximately:

\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{required}}\approx428.
\]

Suppose the first-stage alert has:

- TPR \(=0.80\);
- FPR \(=0.01\).

Its alert-level likelihood ratio is:

\[
\Lambda_A=\frac{0.80}{0.01}=80.
\]

That produces only about **7.4% precision** and leaves a residual evidence factor of:

\[
\frac{428}{80}\approx5.35.
\]

This gives feature discovery a concrete target:

> Do not ask whether a proposed variable is “predictive.” Ask whether it can provide a conditional evidence factor greater than approximately 5.35 inside the existing alert population.

That is a much sharper scientific question.

---

# 3. The correct prediction ceiling

Global Bayes error is a poor primary ceiling for rare events. At prevalence 0.1%, an always-negative classifier has 99.9% accuracy while being operationally worthless.

The appropriate ceiling is decision-specific:

\[
R_X^*(q)
=
\sup_{A\in\sigma(X)}
\left\{
P(A\mid Y=1):
P(Y=1\mid A)\ge q
\right\}.
\]

This is:

> **The maximum recall theoretically achievable using the current information channel \(X\), subject to required precision \(q\).**

An equivalent formulation fixes a false-positive budget:

\[
C_X(\alpha)
=
\sup_{A:P(A\mid Y=0)\le\alpha}
P(A\mid Y=1).
\]

This is the Neyman–Pearson frontier: maximum sensitivity at a controlled FPR. Neyman–Pearson classification and information-theoretic work already provide foundations for cost-sensitive limits, but they have not become the organizing principle of imbalanced-learning practice.

## Four different ceilings must be reported

1. **Receipt-identifiability ceiling**
   How well can the true event be distinguished from the observation policy?

2. **Observed-channel ceiling**
   What is the best decision frontier theoretically available from \(X\)?

3. **Finite-positive ceiling**
   How much of the channel can be learned from the available absolute number of events?

4. **Operational ceiling**
   What can be achieved given action cost, alert capacity, delay, drift, and intervention effects?

Crucially:

> The possible ceiling after adding an unmeasured variable \(Z\) is not identifiable from \(X\) alone.

You cannot infer from a flat model leaderboard that no useful biomarker, device signal, network relation, temporal history, or hidden text cue exists. You must run a targeted measurement pilot.

# 4. The missing-variable hypothesis, made testable

Your strongest instinct is correct: hidden variables will often be concentrated in the error regions.

But “look at false positives and false negatives” is too loose. The precise version is:

> **Candidate variables should be evaluated by their conditional likelihood ratio after matching on the current model score and observation policy.**

For a baseline alert region \(A\) and candidate measurement event \(B\), define:

\[
\operatorname{REG}(B\mid A)
=
\frac{P(B\mid A,Y=1)}
     {P(B\mid A,Y=0)}.
\]

Call this the **Residual Evidence Gain**.

Posterior odds after the second channel become:

\[
\operatorname{Odds}(Y=1\mid A,B)
=
\operatorname{Odds}(Y=1\mid A)
\cdot
\operatorname{REG}(B\mid A).
\]

This is directly estimable in a case-control pilot:

- take true positives in the alert region;
- take score-, time-, site-, and policy-matched apparent negatives;
- acquire candidate \(Z\);
- estimate its conditional evidence gain;
- validate on untouched temporal data.

Existing work can estimate whether a new feature may reduce model loss, and active feature acquisition can choose from already enumerated measurements. The proposed advance is to make feature discovery **rare-event-tail-specific, receipt-aware, and evidence-deficit-targeted**.

# 5. CREDO’s five-stage scaffold

## C — Contract the decision

Before training:

- define the action;
- define prediction time and outcome horizon;
- specify required precision, recall, or FPR;
- specify alert capacity;
- specify false-positive and false-negative costs;
- identify which variables genuinely exist before prediction time.

Calculate:

\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{required}}
=
\frac{q/(1-q)}{\pi/(1-\pi)}.
\]

### Gate C

If the required likelihood ratio is implausibly high for a one-stage model:

- narrow the population;
- raise the prior through upstream screening;
- use a two-stage cascade;
- lower false-positive action cost;
- or abandon individual prediction in favor of group-level or rate forecasting.

---

## R — Repair the receipt

Construct an explicit graph containing:

- true event \(Y\);
- detection or investigation \(O\);
- intervention \(T\);
- recorded label \(\tilde Y\);
- censoring and follow-up;
- human or institutional policy.

Use:

- a random audit stream;
- blinded adjudication;
- repeated or independent ascertainment channels;
- positive–unlabeled models where assumptions are defensible;
- survival or hazard reformulation;
- inverse-propensity methods where observation propensity is estimable.

### Central randomness principle

> **Inject randomness into the observation policy before injecting more randomness into the model.**

Random seeds diversify estimators. Random audits can identify the target.

### Gate R

If recorded labels remain strongly policy-dependent and no audit, instrument, or independent observation channel exists, claims about true-event performance must be treated as partially identified.

---

## E — Estimate the evidence frontier

Use strong, boring baselines first:

- calibrated gradient boosting;
- penalized logistic or generalized additive models;
- natural prevalence during training evaluation;
- temporal and site holdouts;
- threshold optimization only on validation data;
- cluster bootstrap over independent units, not individual rows.

Estimate:

1. learning curves in the absolute positive count \(n_+\);
2. recall at strict FPR budgets;
3. maximum recall at required precision;
4. calibration under natural prevalence;
5. uncertainty caused by the small number of test positives.

For approximate recall \(r\) and desired half-width \(\epsilon\):

\[
n_+
\approx
\frac{z_{0.975}^2r(1-r)}{\epsilon^2}.
\]

At \(r=0.8\) and \(\epsilon=0.05\), approximately **246 independent test positives** are needed before normal-approximation uncertainty alone falls to roughly ±0.05.

### Gate E

Classify the task as:

- **estimation-limited:** frontier rises with more positives;
- **receipt-limited:** label audit changes the target;
- **information-limited:** clean labels, flat learning curve, insufficient frontier;
- **decision-limited:** discrimination exists, but base-rate economics still reject deployment.

Only information-limited tasks proceed to aggressive channel discovery.

---

## D — Discover residual channels

### Step D1: Construct stable residual strata

Use cross-fitted predictions and require errors to persist across credible model families.

Create at least four strata:

1. confident false-positive candidates;
2. confident false negatives;
3. correct positives matched on score;
4. correct negatives matched on score.

Exclude or separately classify:

- future positives;
- label errors;
- intervention-censored outcomes;
- out-of-distribution cases;
- post-outcome leakage.

### Step D2: Run a side-channel test

Before buying new sensors, ask whether signal already exists in:

- free text;
- event sequences;
- device logs;
- timestamps;
- relational or network data;
- ordering behavior;
- repeated measurements;
- geographic or environmental context.

If blinded reviewers can reliably distinguish score-matched positives from negatives using such material, the missing channel may already be recorded but unstructured.

### Step D3: Use the LLM as a constrained hypothesis generator

The LLM receives:

- abstract error descriptions;
- available data schema;
- temporal availability constraints;
- acquisition costs;
- event-mechanism classification;
- prohibited leakage variables.

It proposes:

- direct measurements;
- proxies;
- temporal summaries;
- network variables;
- cross-domain mechanism analogues;
- falsification tests.

It does **not** select the winning variables.

LLM feature engineering, model-aware feature generation, and even residual-driven feature discovery now exist. MedFeat uses downstream-model signals, and a 2026 preprint, CRAFTER, explicitly mines forecast residuals with LLM-generated features. Therefore, “use an LLM to inspect errors” is no longer sufficient as the novelty claim.

The differentiator must be:

> **LLM generation conditioned on the operational evidence deficit, matched rare-event error strata, receipt validity, and false-positive dependence.**

### Step D4: Test candidates cheaply

Measure candidate channels on:

- all available positives;
- score-matched controls;
- a random control sample for population correction.

Primary candidate metric:

\[
\frac{\Delta R_X^*(q)}
     {\text{measurement cost}+\text{action cost}+\text{delay cost}}.
\]

Not:

- global feature importance;
- training AUC;
- in-sample SHAP magnitude;
- or uncorrected F1.

### Gate D

A candidate survives only if it:

- is observable before the decision;
- contributes conditional evidence after matching;
- survives multiplicity control;
- improves the frontier on an untouched temporal set;
- and remains useful after correcting for observation policy.

---

## O — Orthogonalize into an evidence lattice

Ordinary ensembles average models. Rare-event systems should often compose evidence logically.

Let \(m\) index distinct event mechanisms and \(j\) index evidence channels for a mechanism:

\[
\hat Y=1
\iff
\bigvee_{m=1}^{M}
\left[
\bigwedge_{j=1}^{J_m}E_{mj}=1
\right].
\]

Interpretation:

- **AND within a mechanism:** require independent confirmation, increasing specificity.
- **OR across mechanisms:** preserve recall across heterogeneous positive subtypes.

For example:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{alert} ={}&
(\text{behavioral anomaly}\land\text{device anomaly})\\
&\lor(\text{network exposure}\land\text{transaction burst})\\
&\lor(\text{known precursor}\land\text{dynamic instability}).
\end{aligned}
\]

If two tests each have TPR \(0.8\) and FPR \(0.01\), and are conditionally independent within both classes, their conjunction has:

- TPR \(0.64\);
- FPR \(0.0001\);
- approximately 86.5% precision at prevalence 0.1%.

The independence assumption is demanding, but that is exactly why **false-positive dependence must be measured rather than presumed**.

Ensemble theory already shows that diversity is useful only in relation to individual competence and error dependence. CREDO’s addition is to optimize diversity over validated causal or mechanistic channels—not model seeds or architectures.

---

# 6. Confirm and rescue must be separated

A single averaged score often confuses two different jobs.

## Confirmation channels

Applied to high-scoring cases to reject false positives:

\[
A_{\text{confirmed}}=A_{\text{scout}}\land B_{\text{confirm}}.
\]

Optimization target:

\[
\min \mathrm{FPR}
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\mathrm{TPR}\ge r_0.
\]

## Rescue channels

Applied outside the first-stage alert region to find positive subtypes missed by the main model:

\[
A_{\text{final}}
=
A_{\text{confirmed}}
\lor
C_{\text{rescue}}.
\]

Rescue members should be developed around:

- minority mechanisms;
- small positive disjuncts;
- precursor populations;
- network propagation;
- dynamic regime changes;
- special contexts where the main representation fails.

This separates two hypotheses:

- “Why are apparent positives actually negative?”
- “Why are real positives invisible to the main channel?”

They should not be forced into one symmetric loss function.

---

# 7. Correct roles for chaos, randomness, and game theory

## Chaos and dynamical-systems theory

Use only when there is a genuine evolving system with repeat observations.

Appropriate tests include:

- critical slowing down;
- rising variance or autocorrelation;
- reduced recovery rates;
- recurrence structure;
- forecast-horizon decay;
- hidden-state reconstruction.

Critical slowing down has a legitimate literature as an early-warning pattern for some regime transitions, and recent work derives information-theoretic limits for forecasting extreme events in chaotic systems. These tools are inappropriate for unrelated cross-sectional rows.

**Kill test:** If temporal features add no conditional evidence after matching on the current state, classify the event as shock-like rather than transition-like and stop invoking chaos.

## Randomness

Use randomness for:

- random label audits;
- exploration outside the existing alert policy;
- honest uncertainty estimates;
- randomized acquisition pilots;
- sensitivity to data splits.

Do not mistake seed variation for mechanism diversity.

## Game theory

Use only where the positive-generating process adapts:

- fraud;
- intrusion;
- abuse;
- strategic default;
- gaming of inspections;
- adversarial reporting.

The ensemble should prefer channels that are:

- expensive to spoof simultaneously;
- controlled by different actors;
- generated at different times;
- or outside the adversary’s information set.

For non-strategic disease or mechanical failure, game theory should not be the default mathematical language.

# 8. The falsifiable predictions

A breakthrough program needs predictions that can fail.

## Prediction 1: Frontier saturation

On datasets classified as information-limited, resampling, loss modification, and latent augmentation will not materially shift the temporal-holdout recall-at-FPR frontier beyond its uncertainty interval.

## Prediction 2: Residual localization

Candidate measurements discovered from cross-fitted, score-matched residual strata will yield greater validated frontier improvement per candidate than candidates generated from the overall dataset.

## Prediction 3: Evidence-deficit targeting

Features selected by their ability to cover the measured residual likelihood-ratio deficit will outperform features selected by global mutual information, SHAP importance, or global loss reduction.

## Prediction 4: Asymmetric orthogonality

The benefit of a confirmatory channel will be predicted more accurately by its conditional dependence with the existing channel among negatives than by its standalone AUC.

## Prediction 5: Mechanism lattice advantage

An OR-of-ANDs lattice built from validated mechanism channels will achieve lower FPR at matched recall than an averaged ensemble using the same feature and inference budget.

## Prediction 6: Receipt correction

A random audit stream will change the measured precision, calibration, or error composition materially in domains where labels depend on testing or investigation policy.

## Prediction 7: LLM specificity

An LLM conditioned on error mechanism, observability, and evidence deficit will produce more validated feature candidates per dollar than:

- an unconditioned LLM;
- semantic cross-domain analogy;
- conventional automated feature transformations;
- or an equal-budget expert brainstorm.

## Prediction 8: Dynamic-mechanism boundary

Critical-slowing or self-excitation features will help transition and contagion events but not exogenous shock events. Failure to observe this interaction falsifies the mechanism taxonomy.

# 9. CREDO-Bench: the experiment that could establish the field

Create a benchmark for **channel discovery**, not another classifier leaderboard.

## Benchmark construction

Start from richly measured, temporally valid datasets and deliberately hide:

- an entire modality;
- a sensor family;
- a time-history window;
- a relational channel;
- a known precursor;
- or an ascertainment source.

Simulate or reproduce:

- natural imbalance;
- selective labels;
- horizon censoring;
- correlated false-positive channels;
- heterogeneous positive mechanisms;
- temporal and site shift.

## Experimental arms

1. Strong calibrated baseline
2. Resampling and cost-sensitive methods
3. Latent or generative augmentation
4. Conventional feature selection
5. Unconditioned expert feature proposals
6. Unconditioned LLM proposals
7. Model-aware LLM proposals
8. Residual-conditioned proposals
9. Full CREDO: receipt audit + evidence-deficit conditioning + orthogonality selection + mechanism lattice

## Primary endpoints

- recall at certified FPR;
- maximum recall at required precision;
- shift in the decision-specific ceiling;
- hidden-channel recovery rate;
- net utility per acquisition dollar;
- calibration under natural prevalence;
- temporal and cross-site replication.

## Required statistical discipline

- enough independent positives to resolve the expected recall gain;
- enough negatives to certify very low FPRs;
- candidate generation isolated from final evaluation;
- cluster-aware confidence intervals;
- pre-registered candidate and acquisition budgets;
- correction for multiple hypothesis testing;
- no feature proposed using information available only after the prediction time.

---

# 10. The decisive kill test

CREDO should be abandoned or sharply revised if, across several domains:

1. residual-conditioned feature discovery does not outperform global discovery under equal budgets;
2. conditional evidence gain fails to predict out-of-time frontier shift;
3. false-positive dependence does not predict the value of confirmation channels;
4. the mechanism lattice fails to beat a tuned monolithic model or ordinary ensemble;
5. gains vanish after randomized label audits;
6. or channel acquisition costs exceed the value of the resulting decisions.

That is what makes this a scientific hypothesis rather than a consulting framework.

# 11. What is genuinely novel here

The surrounding components already exist independently:

- selective-label correction;
- Bayes and Neyman–Pearson limits;
- active feature acquisition;
- feature-utility prediction;
- LLM feature engineering;
- residual feature discovery;
- ensemble-diversity theory;
- and dynamical early-warning methods.

The defensible novelty is their integration around a new unit of analysis:

> **The residual evidence deficit at the operational decision boundary.**

I did not find a standard framework that jointly:

1. translates prevalence and required precision into a likelihood-ratio deficit;
2. audits whether the label is an event or a policy-generated receipt;
3. discovers new measurements specifically in score-matched error strata;
4. selects channels by conditional evidence and false-positive dependence;
5. composes them as confirmation-within-mechanism and rescue-across-mechanisms;
6. and evaluates progress as movement of a decision-specific frontier per acquisition dollar.

That is the publishable wedge.

# Final one-sentence thesis

> **Imbalanced learning stalled because it tried to manufacture more minority examples inside a fixed and selectively labeled information channel; CREDO instead measures the evidence deficit imposed by the base rate, discovers missing channels precisely where that deficit remains, and combines mechanism-orthogonal evidence so that specificity multiplies without abandoning heterogeneous positives.**

None of these three is daft: they are the same idea seen from three sides, and each maps onto a field that already uses it. This is from memory, with no literature check.

1. Logs and rare errors: the "model organism"
Why it's a good sister problem: In logs you can cause the rare event. Fault injection (chaos engineering) gives true labels with known timing, which receipts never do. You could test H1 to H10 there cheaply before trying them on fraud or disease.
Trick it exports, invariants: Log miners learn rules that always hold in healthy runs (every "open" has a "close"). The receipts version is a violated conservation law, such as a claim with no matching visit.
Trick it exports, the missing line: Often the signal is an expected event that did not happen (a skipped refill, a heartbeat that stops). Row-per-case tables cannot represent absence; sequences can.
Trap: Log errors are often near-deterministic, so methods that shine there may fail in high-overlap domains. Some published log benchmarks have also been criticized as too easy.
2. Recasting as multidimensional time series
What it buys:
Own baseline: Each unit becomes its own control, which removes the persistent "hard negative" that capped the repeated-looks gain.
More looks: You accumulate evidence over time instead of deciding from one snapshot.
Rate targets: You can forecast a hazard rate where individual events are unpredictable.
Fields to borrow from:
Sequential testing (Wald, CUSUM): Accumulate evidence and alarm at a boundary, with known false-alarm-rate maths.
Radar "track-before-detect": Don't threshold each frame; integrate weak signal along plausible paths.
Astronomy difference imaging: Subtract the unit's reference state and look only at the residual.
Trap: Observation timing is itself a receipt, because sicker patients get measured more often. Model the measurement pattern explicitly or it leaks the label.
3. Splitting into solvable and less solvable parts

I think this is the strongest of the three. There are three ways to cut:

By lead time: Earthquakes cannot be predicted, but rate forecasts and seconds-ahead early warning both work. Tornado "watches" (conditions, by region) and "warnings" (detected, minutes ahead) are separate products. Rare-event targets can be split the same way: long-range rate, short-range detection, fast response.
By stage: P(event) = P(exposure) × P(onset | exposure) × P(recorded | onset). Each stage has higher prevalence and its own ceiling, as in fault trees.
By subtype: "The event" is usually a pile of mechanisms. Wear-out failures are predictable; shock failures are not. Predict the first kind and insure against the second.

Trap: Splitting 50 positives into subtypes leaves each with too few, and subtypes found by clustering are forking paths. Define them from mechanism in advance and pool partially across them.

Other sister problems worth raiding
Problem	Trick it exports
Gravitational-wave detection	Time-shift one detector against the other to measure the false-coincidence rate empirically; this would fix the H3 joint false-positive estimate
Particle-physics triggers	Cascades of cheap filters, plus an explicit "look-elsewhere" correction
Flood and extreme-value statistics	Treat the event as the tail of a continuous severity and model the severity
Fuzzing and rare-event simulation	Steer sampling toward failures, then reweight
Aviation safety reporting	No-blame reporting raises the label supply
Drug-safety signal detection	Decades of methods for pure receipts data with no denominator
How your three combine

Convert to trajectories (2), split by lead time and mechanism (3), and prototype on a system where you can inject the event (1). That gives a Gate 0.5 for the earlier plan: a sandbox with known truth.
